Assessment Framework

🔴 Framework Overview

C–G

Emergency Assessment Components

  • Primary Assessment — C-G (life threats first)
  • Secondary Assessment — Subjective history + Head-to-toe
  • Focused System Assessments — Detailed by system
  • Journey — Treatment, transfer, disposition

Goal of ED Assessment

Identify most likely sources of health concerns, detect actual and potential problems, and rapidly initiate life-saving interventions.

Oxygen Therapy Criteria

🫁 O₂ Therapy Criteria

Check
  • SpO₂ < 94%
  • Signs of shock (pale, clammy skin)
  • Abnormal respiratory rate or effort
  • Cyanosis
  • Major Trauma
  • Carbon Monoxide poisoning

<C> CPR / Hemorrhage

First

Assess

  • Signs of life / need for CPR
  • Uncontrolled bleeding

Intervene

  • Start BLS / ACLS / PALS
  • Control bleeding immediately

A — Airway & C-Spine

AVPU

Assess

  • Look, listen, feel for air movement
  • AVPU: Alert · Verbal · Pain · Unresponsive
  • Patency vs obstruction
StridorGaspingSnoring DroolingGurglingUnclear speech

Intervene

  • Suction to clear oropharynx
  • Oral / nasopharyngeal airway
  • Positioning: jaw thrust / chin lift / recovery
  • Spinal Motion Restriction (SMR) if trauma
  • Consider / prepare advanced airway

B — Breathing

Rate · Effort · Quality

Assess

  • Rate, effort, quality of respirations
  • WOB signs: tripoding, retractions, accessory muscles, head-bobbing, grunting
  • Auscultate: bilateral breath sounds (apices & bases)
  • Adventitious sounds: wheezing, crackles

Intervene

  • Assist ventilations: BVM / ventilator
  • Positioning to facilitate breathing
  • Supplemental oxygen (apply O₂ criteria)
  • Pulse oximeter

C — Circulation

Skin · Pulses · ECG

Assess

  • Skin: colour, temperature, moisture
  • Capillary refill time (< 3 sec normal)
  • Palpate central & peripheral pulses: quality, rate, rhythm
  • Chest pain: yes/no (closed-ended)

Intervene

  • Initiate IV(s) or IO
  • Resuscitative fluids
  • Cardiac monitor
  • Stat 12-lead ECG
  • Assess need for defibrillation pads

D — Disability

Neuro · GCS · BGL

Disability (Neuro)

  • Reassess AVPU → GCS if abnormal
  • Pupils: PERRLA
  • Stroke assessment (FAST) if indicated

Dextrose

  • Glucometer reading — consider hypoglycemia treatment

Discomfort

  • Pain assessment — barriers to assessment
  • Vomiting — antiemetic

Doctor

  • Level of Urgency (LOU) — notify physician/specialist

E — Expose

Assess

  • Bruising, wounds, scars, skin colour changes
  • Skin temperature, colour, mottling
  • Drains, tubes, medical devices, medication patches

Intervene

  • Gown and blanket
  • Passive / active warming procedures

F — Full Vitals & Family

  • Full set of vitals (baseline & trending)
  • Check BP bilaterally if appropriate
  • Check for postural drop if appropriate
  • Facilitate family presence
  • Determine frequency of vital sign reassessment

G — Go Back & Reassess

⚠️ Critical Rule

Do not proceed to the next step until interventions for life-threatening conditions have been implemented and re-evaluated.

  • Reassess impact of interventions on red flags
  • Re-evaluate need for further intervention
  • Reassess C–F: Are interventions effective?

👶 PAT — Pediatric Assessment Triangle

Peds Only

Appearance — TICLS

  • Tone — muscle tone
  • Interactivity — interaction with environment
  • Consolability — response to caregiver
  • Look — tracks things appropriately?
  • Speech/Cry — vocalization quality

Work of Breathing

  • Abnormal airway sounds
  • Positioning

Circulation to Skin

  • Pallor, mottling, cyanosis

Across-the-room first look assessment — performed before approaching patient.

Pediatric Primary: <C> → G

Same C–G Framework as Adults

Follow the same primary assessment steps. Key differences are noted in the Advanced tab and CIAMPEDS history (Secondary).

Additional Peds Considerations

  • Weight in kg — essential for all drug/fluid calculations
  • Use age-appropriate AVPU criteria
  • PEWS vital sign norms differ by age (see Reference tab)
  • Fontanelle assessment if <18 months: bulging / depressed

Advanced C — Circulation

Advanced

Additional Assessments

  • Heart sounds
  • Signs of hemorrhage
  • eFAST / POCUS ultrasound
  • Pelvis stability

Advanced Interventions

  • Balanced crystalloid fluids (1–2 L)
  • Blood products 1:1:1 (PRBC : Plasma : Platelets)
  • Tranexamic Acid (TXA)
  • Pericardial needle aspiration
  • Pelvic binder
  • Control bleeding — tourniquet

Advanced B — Breathing

Additional Interventions

  • BiPAP
  • High-Flow Nasal Cannula (Optiflow / Airvo)
  • Needle thoracostomy / chest tube

I — Inspect Posterior (Advanced)

  • Log roll to inspect back
  • Doctor performs rectal exam

RSI — Rapid Sequence Intubation

1

Preparation

Equipment, team, backup plan

2

Preoxygenate

Maximize O₂ stores prior to intubation

3

Pretreatment

Atropine, lidocaine (where indicated)

4

Paralysis & Induction

Sedative + neuromuscular blockade simultaneously

5

Protection & Position

Cricoid pressure, optimal positioning

6

Proof of Placement

ETCO₂, bilateral breath sounds, CXR

7

Post-Intubation Care

Vent settings, sedation, re-assess

Ventilator Modes

Assist Control Volume (AC-VC)

Full support. Set: RR, Tidal Volume (Vt), PEEP, FiO₂

Assist Control Pressure (AC-PC)

Full support. Set: RR, Pressure, PEEP, FiO₂

Pressure Support (PS)

Patient initiates all breaths. Set: Pressure Support, PEEP, FiO₂

Titration Rule

  • Change pH / pCO₂ → adjust Vt / RR (ventilation)
  • Change PaO₂ / SpO₂ → adjust FiO₂ / PEEP (oxygenation)

LOTARP — History of Presenting Illness

L

Location / Chief Complaint

Where is the problem? If location N/A, use CC (SOB, seizure, dizziness, weakness, nausea).

O

Onset

Sudden or gradual? How long has this been going on?

T

Type / Timing

  • Constant or intermittent?
  • Pain: sharp, stabbing, crushing, scale
  • Seizure: focal, tonic-clonic, absence
  • Vision: blurred, double, floaters
A

Alleviating / Aggravating & Associated Symptoms

What makes it better or worse? ROS — what else? Pertinent negatives.

R

Radiation

Does it affect or move to another area? (May not apply to all CCs)

P

Precipitating Event

Circumstances prior to / surrounding onset of problem.

Remaining Subjective History

  • Allergies / sensitivities
  • Medications (current)
  • Past Health History — pertinent to CC, family tendencies, risk factors
  • Last Meal
  • Environment
  • Substance use / ETOH / smoking
  • Safety at home / risk of domestic violence

Review of Systems (ROS)

Systems to Consider

  • General: Weight change, mood, weakness, fatigue
  • Integumentary: Skin problems, rashes, bruising
  • Neurological: Headache, LOC, dizziness, slurred speech, numbness, tremors
  • HEENT: Visual changes, hearing, sore throat, neck stiffness
  • Cardiovascular: Palpitations, syncope, chest pain, dyspnea, edema
  • Respiratory: Dyspnea, cough, sputum, hemoptysis, sick contacts
  • GI: N/V, heartburn, appetite, hematemesis, melena, bowel habits
  • GU: Frequency, urgency, hematuria, pain, STI exposure
  • Reproductive: LMP, gravida/para, contraception, sexual activity
  • MSK: Muscle/joint pain, arthritis, injuries
  • Mental Health: Depression, anxiety, SI/HI, hallucinations

👶 CIAMPEDS — Pediatric History

C

Chief Complaint

Reason for ED visit and duration (e.g., fever × 2 days).

I

Immunizations / Isolation

  • Current immunization status
  • Exposure to communicable diseases
  • Immunocompromised?
A

Allergies

Medications, foods, products, environmental

M

Medications

Prescription and OTC: dose, time of last dose, duration

P

Past Medical History / Parent Impression

  • Prior illness, injuries, hospitalizations, surgeries
  • <1 yr: prenatal/birth history, birth weight, NICU
  • Menarche: LMP date and description
  • Sexually active: contraception, STI hx, G/P
  • Caregiver concerns and cultural context
E

Events Surrounding Illness / Injury

  • Illness: onset, progression, sick contacts, pre-treatment
  • Injury: time, mechanism, witnessed?, prehospital Tx, VS
D

Diet / Diapers

  • Last meal and fluid intake
  • Changes in eating patterns
  • Urine: frequency, colour, odour (last 24h)
  • Last BM, colour, consistency, frequency change
S

Symptoms Associated with Illness / Injury

Identification and progression of symptoms since onset.

General Appearance

  • Affect, behaviour, hygiene
  • Pain / anxiety / level of distress

Head & Neck

Inspect

  • Signs of trauma, bruising
  • Nose & ears for blood or drainage
  • Mouth and tongue: foreign bodies, swelling, oral care
  • Facial symmetry
  • Tracheal position (midline?)

Palpate

  • Scalp and facial bones — pain / abnormalities
  • Cervical spine — pain or tenderness

Chest

Inspect

  • Signs of trauma, bruising
  • Expansion/retraction — symmetry, WOB, accessory muscles, paradoxical movement

Palpate

  • Clavicles, sternum, lateral chest wall — deformities, pain, crepitus, subcutaneous emphysema

Auscultate

  • Bilateral air entry: anteriorly / posteriorly, apices & bases
  • Adventitious sounds
  • Apex rate, regularity, clarity, extra heart sounds

Abdomen

Inspect

  • Signs of trauma, bruising
  • Distension or asymmetry
  • Pulsations
  • Genitalia: bleeding, bruising, incontinence

Auscultate

  • Bowel sounds: present, hyper/hypoactive, absent

Palpate & Compress

  • Abdomen: distention, firmness, masses, pain
  • Iliac crest: pain, crepitus, instability
  • Symphysis pubis: pain, crepitus, instability

Extremities (Bilateral)

Inspect

  • Edema, deformity, pressure areas, trauma
  • Position of extremities
  • Skin colour and integrity

Palpate / Test

  • Pain, deformity, ROM
  • Radial, DP, PT pulses
  • Cap refill < 3 seconds
  • Handgrip strength (equality)
  • Dorsiflexion and plantar flexion (strength and equality)

Vertebral Area

⚠️ SMR

Maintain Spinal Motion Restriction if potential for spinal injury exists.

  • Inspect spinal column for deformity / trauma
  • Palpate para-vertebral / vertebral area: pain or muscle spasm

❤️ Cardiovascular

Inspect

  • Skin colour and condition
  • Edema: extremity, sacral, facial

Palpate

  • Pulses: rate, regularity, quality, equality (radial, femoral, PT, DP)
  • Skin: temperature, texture, turgor
  • Edema: location, severity (mild/mod/severe), pitting vs non-pitting
  • JVD (measurement optional)

Auscultate

  • BP bilaterally (if appropriate)
  • Heart rate and rhythm
  • Pulse deficit: apical vs radial comparison
  • Heart sounds: quality (clear/distant/muffled)
  • S1 and S2
  • Extra sounds: S3, S4, rubs, murmurs, gallops

🫁 Respiratory

Anterior AND posterior chest

Inspect

  • Client position: tripoding?
  • Skin / earlobes / nail beds / mouth colour
  • Cough and expectorate: colour, amount, consistency
  • Rate, rhythm, depth of breathing
  • WOB: accessory muscles, tracheal deviation/tugging
  • Chest deformities, scars, injuries
  • Symmetry of chest movement

Palpate

  • Areas of tenderness
  • Crepitus / subcutaneous emphysema

Auscultate

  • Air entry presence and depth
  • Normal vs adventitious: crackles, wheezes, pleural friction rub

🦴 Musculoskeletal / Integumentary

MSK — Inspect

  • Extremity position (abnormal flexion, extension, shortening, rotation, ROM)
  • Deformities, swelling
  • Colour: pale, reddened, cyanotic

MSK — Palpate

  • Pain, tenderness, crepitus
  • Pulses: presence, strength, equality
  • Sensation (paresthesia)
  • Temperature equality, strength equality

  • Colour, lesions, scars, drainage
  • Lacerations, contusions, abrasions, burns, pressure injuries
  • Nails, mucous membranes, wounds

Integumentary — Palpate

  • Moisture, temperature, turgor, texture

🫃 Abdominal

1. Inspect

  • Trauma, bruising, injury
  • Pulsating masses, symmetry, herniation
  • Visible peristalsis

2. Auscultate

  • Bowel sounds: present, hyper/hypoactive, absent
  • Fetal heart rate (if applicable)

3. Percuss

  • CVA (costo-vertebral angle) for flank pain

4. Palpate

  • Tenderness over 4 quadrants
  • Guarding or rigidity
  • Masses / pulsations

🧠 Neurological

Pupillary Response

  • Equal size, Round, Reactive to light (consensual)
  • Accommodation, Extraocular Movements (EOMs)

Stroke Assessment

  • Facial droop
  • Arm drift
  • Movement / strength of limbs × 4
  • Slurred speech

🚀 Journey

Final Step

An ever-evolving process. Prioritize short & long-term clinical goals systematically.

Diagnostics

  • Imaging: CT / X-Ray / Ultrasound
  • Lab work

Transfer / Higher Level of Care

  • Intra/inter-hospital transfer
  • Specialty services
  • OR / Interventional radiology

Planning & Preparation

  • Tubes & lines: NG/OG, catheter, etc.
  • Team communication priorities
  • Consults: physiotherapy, OT, geriatrician

Home / Discharge Planning

  • Access to follow-up care & medications
  • Ability to attend to ADLs / IADLs at home
  • Family / social support networks
  • Start discharge teaching early

🩸 Massive Transfusion Protocol

1:1:1

1:1:1 Ratio

  • PRBCs — ↑ O₂ carrying capacity, ↑ volume
  • Plasma — ↑ volume, provides clotting factors
  • Platelets — ↑ volume, provides clotting factors, albumin

Equal amounts of each component — not necessarily equal number of bags.

Trauma Diamond of Death

Hypothermia Acidosis Coagulopathy Hypocalcemia

Additional Agents

  • TXA (Tranexamic Acid) — prevent clot breakdown
  • Cryoprecipitate, Octaplex — clotting support
  • Replace calcium per hospital protocol (prevent hypocalcemia)

📈 Oxyhemoglobin Dissociation Curve

Shift LEFT ← (↑ affinity — holds O₂)

O₂ bound to Hb more tightly → less available to tissues

↓ Temperature↓ pCO₂↓ H⁺ ions

Shift RIGHT → (↓ affinity — releases O₂)

O₂ unloaded easily → stores utilized quickly

↑ Temperature↑ pCO₂↑ H⁺ ions

Normal PaO₂ > 80 mmHg

📝 Charting Examples

On Arrival — Fever (UTI)

Pt presents via w/c with 2-day hx of fever, malaise, and generalized muscle aches. Paraplegic, self-catheterizes 4×/day. Foul-smelling urine ×3 days; denies blood/dark urine. Decreased fluid intake ×2 days. Denies flank pain. Hx UTIs — last 2 months ago treated with cipro. Denies abdominal pain, N/V/D. Denies cough, SOB, sick contacts. Tylenol 650 mg BID ×2 days — no effect. O/A: skin flushed, hot, dry. Anxious. Moderate ↑ WOB.

On Arrival — Chest Pain (ACS)

Pt presents with 5-hr hx of chest pain, nausea, lightheadedness. Post-flight from China (11 hrs ago). ASA 300 mg given by EHS. Pain: central, sudden onset, constant 7/10, "heavy pressure," radiates to jaw and L arm. Denies SOB, cough, fever, calf pain/swelling. Nausea ×5 hrs. Slightly dizzy since pain onset. No HA, visual changes, or trauma. O/A: alert, sitting up on stretcher, resps easy, skin normal for ethnicity.

Shift Handover — Airway/Breathing/Circulation Format

(A) Airway patent and protected. (B) Normal WOB. Breath sounds clear to bases. SpO₂ 98% RA. (C) NSR. No chest pain. Peripheral pulses strong, regular. Skin dry, normal colour and temperature. Patent saline lock L arm — site satisfactory. (D) Alert & oriented ×3, mentating well. (GI) Abdomen soft, BS ×4 quadrants. (GU) No flank pain. (MSK) No skin breakdown. Patient states feeling better post fluid bolus. Awaiting MD review.
